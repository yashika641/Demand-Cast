# 1️⃣ **DEMAND FORECASTING — FULL DETAILED KNOWLEDGE**

Demand forecasting predicts **how much of a product customers will buy in the future**, using historical patterns and influencing factors.

It is the **foundation** of all supply-chain planning.

---

## 🔹 **What is Demand?**

Demand is the quantity of goods or services consumers are willing to purchase under certain:

* prices
* time periods
* market conditions
* availability

Demand reflects **customer intention + purchasing power**.

---

## 🔹 **Why Forecast Demand?** (Business Purpose)

* Plan inventory levels
* Prevent stockouts
* Avoid overstock (dead stock cost)
* Reduce working capital
* Optimize procurement
* Schedule transportation
* Predict revenue
* Allocate budgets
* Manage promotions & pricing strategies

DemandCast uses automated ML to forecast demand at **SKU × Location × Day** granularity.

---

## 🔹 **Types of Demand Patterns**

1. **Level Demand** – consistent demand
2. **Trend Demand** – increasing/decreasing demand
3. **Seasonal Demand** – recurring patterns (weekends, holidays, festivals)
4. **Cyclic Demand** – irregular but repeating over business cycles
5. **Irregular/Random Demand** – unpredictable spikes or dips

---

## 🔹 **Demand Features (Predictor Variables)**

Typical features include:

### **Time-based**

* Day of Week
* Month
* Week number
* Quarter
* Holiday flags
* Weekend flags

### **Demand History**

* Lag features: `lag_1`, `lag_7`, `lag_30`
* Moving averages: 7-day, 30-day
* Rolling standard deviation
* Rolling maximum/minimum

### **Price Features**

* Regular price
* Discount %
* Price difference
* Competitor price

### **External Features**

* Weather
* Events
* Promotion calendars
* Stock levels

DemandCast supports flexible feature engineering.

---

## 🔹 **Forecasting Horizons**

Forecasting horizon = how far you predict into the future.

Short-term: 1–7 days
Medium-term: 7–30 days
Long-term: 30–180 days

Each horizon requires different model behavior.

---

## 🔹 **Forecasting Methods (Detailed)**

### **1. Classical Statistical Methods**

#### 🔸 ARIMA

Autoregressive Integrated Moving Average

* Good for univariate time series
* Captures trend + autocorrelation

#### 🔸 Exponential Smoothing (ETS)

* Uses smoothing factors
* Great for level + seasonality

#### 🔸 Prophet

* Built by Facebook
* Automatically handles trend, seasonality, holidays

---

### **2. Machine Learning Methods**

#### 🔸 Linear Regression

* Used when features are linear
* Fast and interpretable

#### 🔸 Random Forest

* Good for non-linear relationships
* Handles many features

#### 🔸 XGBoost

* Gradient boosting
* High accuracy for tabular data
* Works well with:

  * price effects
  * promotions
  * engineered features

DemandCast uses XGBoost for **large datasets**.

---

### **3. Deep Learning Methods**

#### 🔸 LSTM (Long Short-Term Memory)

Specialized neural networks for sequential data.
Best for:

* Long-term patterns
* Complex temporal relationships
* Multi-dimensional forecasting

---

## 🔹 **Forecast Evaluation Metrics**

| Metric | What It Means                               |
| ------ | ------------------------------------------- |
| MAE    | Average absolute error; easy to understand  |
| RMSE   | Penalizes large errors; sensitive to spikes |
| MAPE   | Percentage error; scale-free                |
| R²     | How much variance is explained              |

DemandCast returns all metrics per model.

# 2️⃣ **STOCKOUT PREDICTION — FULL DETAILED KNOWLEDGE**

Stockout prediction estimates whether inventory will go to **zero** within a future time window.

Stockouts cause:

* lost revenue
* lost customers
* damaged brand trust
* lower service level
* emergency procurement cost

---

## 🔹 **What is a Stockout?**

A stockout occurs when:

```
Inventory Available = 0 AND Customer Demand > 0
```

---

## 🔹 **Stockout Causes (Root Level)**

1. Poor forecasting
2. Supplier delays
3. Low safety stock
4. High demand spikes
5. Promotions with insufficient stock
6. Incorrect reorder calculations
7. Long or variable lead times

---

## 🔹 **Stockout Prediction Problem Type**

It is a **binary classification task**:

```
1 → Stockout will occur
0 → Stockout unlikely
```

---

## 🔹 **Feature Engineering for Stockout Models**

* Current stock level
* Average daily demand
* Rolling demand volatility
* Lead time lag features
* Incoming purchase orders
* Supplier reliability score
* Safety stock
* Price days
* Promotion indicators

Stockout risk is a function of:

```
Risk = f(demand_rate, stock_level, lead_time_variability)
```

---

## 🔹 **ML Models Commonly Used**

* Logistic Regression
* RandomForestClassifier
* XGBoostClassifier
* LSTM (when lag features exist)

XGBoost is preferred for:

* imbalance learning
* high dimensional data
* non-linear patterns

---

## 🔹 **Class Imbalance Issue**

Stockouts are **rare events** → only 1–5% of records are stockouts.

Solutions:

* SMOTE
* Undersampling
* Class weighting
* Balanced subsampling

DemandCast applies automated imbalance handling.

---

## 🔹 **Business Outputs**

* Probability of stockout
* Expected stockout date
* Stock risk score (0–1)
* RAG chatbot explanation

# 3️⃣ **LEAD TIME ANALYTICS — FULL DETAILED KNOWLEDGE**

Lead time determines **when replenishment stock will arrive**.

Without understanding lead time, you cannot:

* calculate reorder points
* plan safety stock
* prevent stockouts
* plan logistics

---

## 🔹 **Lead Time Definition**

Time taken from ordering a product to receiving it into inventory.

General formula:

```
Lead Time = Delivery Date − Order Date
```

---

## 🔹 **Stages in Lead Time**

1. PO creation
2. Supplier acceptance
3. Manufacturing time
4. Packaging
5. Outbound logistics
6. Transit
7. Customs
8. Warehouse receiving

---

## 🔹 **Lead Time Variability**

This is the *most important factor* affecting stockouts.

Variability sources:

* Supplier unreliability
* Transportation delays
* Weather disruptions
* Raw material shortages

DemandCast includes:

* variability smoothing
* anomaly detection for extreme delays

---

## 🔹 **Lead Time Model**

A regression model predicting:

* expected lead time (in days)
* lead time variability (std deviation)
* delay probability

Used for **smart reorder point calculation**.

# 4️⃣ **PRICE ELASTICITY – FULL DETAILED KNOWLEDGE**

Price elasticity measures **how sensitive customers are to price changes**.

---

## 🔹 **Elasticity Definition**

```
Elasticity = % Change in Quantity / % Change in Price
```

Elastic: >1
Inelastic: <1
Unit elastic: =1

---

## 🔹 **Types of Elasticity**

* **Own-price elasticity**: effect of your product's price
* **Cross-price elasticity**: effect of competitor product price
* **Income elasticity**: effect of consumer income

DemandCast supports:

* log-log elasticity
* regression elasticity
* cross-elasticity matrix

---

## 🔹 **Elastic vs Inelastic Products**

**Elastic examples:**

* Luxury goods
* Electronics
* Fashion
* Non-essential food

**Inelastic examples:**

* Salt
* Milk
* Medicines
* Petrol

---

## 🔹 **Revenue Maximizing Price**

Revenue formula:

```
Revenue = Price × Demand
```

Optimal price occurs where:

```
Elasticity = -1
```

DemandCast generates:

* demand curve
* revenue curve
* optimal price point

# 5️⃣ **PROMOTIONAL EFFECTIVENESS – FULL DETAILED KNOWLEDGE**

Promotions temporarily increase sales through:

* discounts
* cashback
* BOGO
* bundles
* limited-time offers

---

## 🔹 **Key Concepts**

### **Baseline Sales**

Sales expected WITHOUT promo.

### **Promo Sales**

Actual sales during promo.

### **Uplift**

```
Uplift = Promo Sales − Baseline Sales
```

### **Incremental Revenue**

```
Incremental Revenue = Uplift × Promo Price
```

### **Promo ROI**

```
ROI = (Incremental Profit − Promo Cost) / Promo Cost
```

---

## 🔹 **Promo Effectiveness Drivers**

1. Discount depth
2. Promotion type (BOGO, flat, % off)
3. Days of promotion
4. Seasonality
5. Competitor activity
6. Location differences

---

## 🔹 **Why Promos Fail**

* Offered on low-demand items
* No stock to support the promo
* Cannibalization
* Excessive discounts
* Too frequent promos → fatigue

# 6️⃣ **SUPPLY CHAIN CONCEPTS — FULL DETAIL**

### **Safety Stock**

Extra inventory to protect against uncertainty.

Formula:

```
Safety Stock = Z × σ(Demand during Lead Time)
```

### **Reorder Point (ROP)**

```
ROP = Demand × Lead Time + Safety Stock
```

### **Fill Rate**

% of demand served without delay.

### **Backorders**

Orders placed when item is out of stock.

### **Dead Stock**

Inventory that will not sell.

### **Cycle Stock**

Inventory used to meet regular demand.

### **Pipeline Inventory**

Inventory ordered but not yet received.
